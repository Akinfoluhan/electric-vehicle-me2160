# Electric Vehicle — Intro to ME Design Project

Electrically powered vehicle designed, fabricated, and tested for ME2160 (Intro to Mechanical Engineering Design). Completed the 10-ft run in 0.60 seconds and placed first in the speed category.

## Overview

This project involved designing and building a small electrically powered vehicle using a standard kit of parts and a custom chassis. The vehicle was required to fit within an 8 in × 8 in × 8 in volume and complete a 10-ft run without external assistance.

Our design focused on speed and consistency. We selected a drivetrain and wheel configuration aimed at maximizing acceleration over the short track length while staying within the project constraints.

## Requirements and Constraints

- Vehicle must fit within an 8 in × 8 in × 8 in volume.
- Vehicle must travel 10 ft on its own (no external push/pull).
- Chassis constructed from 1/8 in plywood (laser-cut), with kit-provided mechanical/electrical components.
- Drivetrain and transmission choices must follow the available kit parts and assembly guidelines.

## Design Approach

We iterated from an initial tractor-pull concept to a speed-focused vehicle after early drivetrain and packaging constraints made the original direction less competitive. The final design prioritized efficient power transfer, low mechanical losses, and stable gear engagement over a short 10-ft run.

Key design decisions:

- Reduced friction at the axle by using a low-friction mounting approach to minimize energy loss.
- Used a compact spur-gear drivetrain and targeted a low gear ratio (3:1) based on the kit constraints and speed goal.
- Revised the frame and axle-gear slot geometry to prevent the axle gear from sliding out of mesh; the slot was rotated and narrowed to keep the gear consistently engaged.
- Improved electrical reliability with a soldered wiring setup (battery, switch, motor).
- Increased motor input voltage by wiring two batteries in series (18 V total) and placed them symmetrically on the frame for balanced weight distribution.

This combination of mechanical iteration and electrical optimization led to a fast, repeatable final build.

## Results and Performance

- Outcome: our team won the fastest vehicle category.
- Track performance: completed the 10-ft run in 0.60 seconds.
- Reliability: after iterating on drivetrain alignment and gear engagement, the final build consistently completed the run without slipping out of mesh.
